This is a single-major program — students study the full computer science curriculum, and alongside it build a deep foundation in biology, chemistry, and biomedical statistics. The result: graduates who can write the algorithms driving the next generation of drug discovery, genome analysis, personalized medicine, and synthetic biology.

## Why now

Biology has become a data-rich science. DNA sequencing, high-resolution cell imaging, continuous physiological measurements from smartwatches — all generate more information than humans can analyze by hand. The tools that decode that data — AI models, statistical algorithms, computational methods — are at the heart of modern biomedical research. Graduates of this program are the ones who build those tools.

## What you'll study

Three years, 160 credits, a clear split between computer science foundations and biology/chemistry:

**Year 1 — Foundations (61 credits).** Core computer science: introduction, data structures, C/C++. Mathematics: calculus, linear algebra, discrete mathematics. Biology and chemistry: general chemistry, organic chemistry, genetics, introduction to biology.

**Year 2 — The toolkit (48 credits).** Algorithms, introduction to machine learning, object-oriented programming, applied mathematics and statistics. Biology: molecular biology, cell biochemistry, physics for computational biology students. Plus: an introductory course in computational biology.

**Year 3 — Specialization and research (29 credits + electives).** Computational models, computability and complexity, operating systems, computational biology workshop, advanced biostatistics, and directed research in molecular computational biology. You choose electives by direction.

## Who it's for

Students drawn to life sciences but unwilling to give up the depth and rigor of computer science. Students who want to understand the scientific basis of the tools transforming biomedicine. Students aiming at careers in drug discovery, bioinformatics, genome analysis, or biological engineering.

## What comes after

Graduates continue to M.Sc. and Ph.D. programs in computational biology, bioinformatics, or computer science; move into research roles at biomedical research institutes in Israel and abroad; or join biotech and medtech companies leading the next wave of data-driven medicine.
