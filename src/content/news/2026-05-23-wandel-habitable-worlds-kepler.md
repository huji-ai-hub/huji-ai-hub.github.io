A new study by Dr. Amri Wandel of the Institute of Physics at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem challenges the conventional focus on Earth-like planets in narrow habitable zones. The research, published in *The Astrophysical Journal*, suggests that the range of worlds capable of supporting basic conditions for life may be significantly wider than current estimates.

The study is based on a fresh analysis of data from NASA's Kepler Space Telescope, which discovered thousands of exoplanets and for the first time allowed statistical estimates of how common small planets are around different types of stars.

By broadening the criteria for habitability beyond strict Earth-analog conditions, Wandel's work opens the possibility that life-supporting environments could be much more prevalent across the galaxy.
